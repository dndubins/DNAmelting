## DNAmelting
A collection of melting curve programs in Rproject and MS Excel

In a recent workshop, the instructor said "if it looks like you have the right answer, it's probably right."<p>
Confirmation bias is unfortunately everywhere in modeling. On my first day of graduate school, as I entered the lab to meet my labmates, I saw a large banner printed on track paper, taped over the fume hood. It said in all caps:<p>
<s1> EVERYTHING YOU KNOW IS WRONG</s1><p>
Contained in this repository is a series of models. When modeling, remember that our understanding of the world is limited. A clever AI once told me, "the physical world is under no obligation to respect your abstractions." Notwithstanding, modeling is fun and informative. It can be frustrating, finicky, and at times obsessive. It's important to at least start with good clean data, or the parameters won't mean anything. That means if you are looking at an ugly curve where it's clear something bad happened, don't bother fitting it. It won't help you. It's a good model of when things go wrong.
